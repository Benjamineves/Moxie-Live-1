Brand Context

Essential brand positioning and audience context for anyone designing Moxie materials.

Moxie is a premium marine technology and services company building the digital identity layer for yachts and vessels. The core product is a QR-based "digital dry bag" — a persistent digital profile for every boat, accessible by scanning a weatherproof sticker on the hull. Think of it as the boat equivalent of a car's glove box, but digital, always current, and role-gated by who scans it.

Who we serve

Moxie's primary customers are affluent yacht owners — typically high-net-worth individuals who own vessels valued at $150K–$2M+. These are people who expect the same digital sophistication from their boating experience that they get from luxury automotive, private aviation, or premium real estate. They are accustomed to brands like Hermès, Porsche, and Four Seasons. Design that looks "tech startup" or "SaaS dashboard" will immediately feel wrong to this audience.

Secondary customers include marina operators (enterprise B2B), yacht brokerages, marine title/escrow companies, and yacht clubs. These audiences expect professionalism and authority, not playfulness.

Brand personality

Moxie should feel like a private yacht club's concierge desk, not a tech company's landing page. The brand sits at the intersection of nautical heritage and modern digital precision. It is confident and quiet — never shouty, never gimmicky. The gold-and-navy palette evokes brass hardware, teak decks, and midnight water. Typography is editorial and elegant, with serif headings that feel like a luxury lifestyle magazine, not a startup pitch deck.

Key brand attributes: Authoritative. Elegant. Trustworthy. Premium. Maritime.

What Moxie is NOT: playful, techy, startup-y, casual, minimalist-corporate, or Silicon Valley. Never use rounded sans-serif logos, gradient backgrounds, or tech-bro aesthetics.

The digital dry bag

Moxie's signature product is the QR sticker system. A single weatherproof vinyl sticker goes on the stern, dock box, or companionway of a vessel. When scanned, it shows a role-gated digital profile: the general public sees vessel specs, the marina operator sees emergency contacts and insurance, the owner sees everything and can edit. Coast Guard and first responders see critical safety data.

This QR sticker is a physical branding touchpoint that lives on $200K+ yachts. It must look premium — it sits alongside the vessel's name decal and hull graphics. Think of it as jewelry for the boat, not a shipping label.

Geographic context

Moxie is headquartered in Northern California, with initial deployment in the San Francisco Bay Area — specifically Emery Cove Marina (Emeryville) and Sausalito/Clipper Yacht Harbor. The Bay Area yachting community is affluent, environmentally conscious, and tech-literate but design-discerning. Future expansion targets the Pacific Northwest and Florida.

Competitive positioning

There is no direct competitor doing what Moxie does. The closest analogs are BoatCloud (basic vessel documentation) and YachtWorld (listings marketplace). Both are utilitarian and ugly. Moxie's design advantage is a core differentiator — the brand should feel like it belongs next to Boat International magazine, not next to a government registry website.

Typography Rules

Headlines: Always Cormorant Garamond. Use Light 300 weight for large display. Italic for emphasis and vessel names. Gold accent on key words using <em> or <span> styling.

Body text: Always DM Sans. Never use Cormorant for body paragraphs. Default weight is 400 at 14–15px with generous line-height (1.7–1.8).

Labels/eyebrows: DM Sans Medium 500, 10–11px, heavy letter-spacing (0.16–0.22em), always uppercase. Often flanked by gold dashes or decorative lines.

Vessel names: Cormorant Garamond Light Italic. Always italic. This is the editorial "boat name" treatment used across all touchpoints.

CTAs/buttons: DM Sans, 11–12px, uppercase, letter-spacing 0.12–0.18em. Font-weight 500–600.

Decorative Patterns

Gold radial glow: Used as subtle background decoration on navy sections. radial-gradient(circle, rgba(201,168,76,0.06-0.12) 0%, transparent 70%) — positioned top-right or offset for asymmetry.

Gold line accents: Thin gold lines (rgba(201,168,76,0.35)) used as dividers, eyebrow flanking dashes, and animated underlines on hover.

Glass nav: Navigation uses backdrop-filter: blur(12px) with semi-transparent cream background rgba(245,242,236,0.92).

Card hover treatment: Cards on dark backgrounds use a gold line that scales from left on hover: transform: scaleX(0) → scaleX(1) with gold background.

Border radius: Large cards use 12–16px radius. Badges/pills use 20px. Stickers use 10–14px. Phone mockup uses 36px. No sharp corners anywhere in the UI.

Design Rules

Do

Use Cormorant Garamond italic for all vessel names. Use generous whitespace — let the design breathe. Pair navy backgrounds with gold accents. Use the cream palette for light sections (never pure white backgrounds on full pages). Keep buttons uppercase with wide letter-spacing. Use editorial photography language (if/when imagery is used). Think Boat International, Robb Report, Condé Nast Traveller.

Don't

Never use gradients (except the subtle gold radial glow). Never use rounded sans-serif logos or "tech startup" aesthetics. Never use emoji in formal materials. Never use Inter, Roboto, or system default fonts for headings. Never put the wordmark in anything other than Cormorant Garamond Italic. Never use the gold as a full background color (except for QR sticker variant). Never use navy text on navy backgrounds. Never crowd the layout — if it feels like a SaaS dashboard, it's wrong.

Key terminology

"Digital dry bag" — the official product metaphor. Never use "digital glove box" (deprecated). A dry bag is what sailors use to keep critical documents safe on a boat — it's the perfect metaphor for what Moxie does digitally.

"Vessel profile" — not "boat page" or "listing." Moxie profiles are persistent identities, not marketplace listings.

"Moxie" — always capitalized. Never "MOXIE" in all-caps except in QR sticker labels where it's part of the uppercase design language.
